Lightscapes of Fear

Behavioral Rest at Sea

This interactive map displays the tracks, trip duration, and dive metadata for 8 of the 71 female northern elephant seals incorporated in a study investigating the risk-reward tradeoffs of behavioral rest at sea.

Data from time-depth recorder and satellite instruments were processed in MATLAB and converted into KML files. Individual KML files were imported into a single KMZ zipped file to be imported into Google Maps' My Maps tool. Metadata was exported from MATLAB into a CSV and paired to the data layer containing each respective animal's track.

This interactive data visualization was created to accompany the Science Advances publication "Lightscapes of Fear: How mesopredators balance starvation and predation in the open ocean."

Map, infographics, and cover art by Jessica Kendall-Bar. Data analysis by Roxanne Beltran. Illustrations by Danielle Dube, Art-Science Residency with the Norris Center for Natural History.

Pick a Seal

Click on a female northern elephant seal's track in the interactive map to learn her TOPPID, trip duration, departure date, arrival date, number of rest dives, and total number of dives.

Follow Her Journey

While at sea, elephant seals perform transit dives, benthic dives, foraging dives, and drift dives. Drift dives are dives where they swim down to 200–300 meters, turn upside down, and drift for several minutes before swimming back to the surface.

As the seal gains body fat, her body composition shifts and she becomes less dense. Once she gains enough fat to become positively buoyant, she starts to float up toward the surface instead of sinking to the safety of darkness. She shifts her sleep schedule earlier so she can sleep in darkness, sacrificing valuable hours of nighttime foraging. She also begins her drift dives deeper so she ends at about the same depth as if negatively buoyant. By tracking buoyancy over time, the study tracks risk-taking behavior over months at sea.

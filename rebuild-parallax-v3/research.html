Research

My research combines engineering, data science, ecology, and visualization to measure behavior and physiology of marine animals in a changing climate. I design tools that make invisible animal behavior and physiology visible — from biosensors and biologging systems to data-driven animation, AI-assisted analysis, and decision-support visualization.

EcoViz Lab

The new site should send research collaborators, students, and funders directly to the lab website: EcoViz Lab. This personal site can function as the archive and portfolio; the lab site should be the primary home for current lab projects, students, publications, and recruitment.

Research pathways

  • Biologging & engineering — engineer new biosensors and data workflows.
  • Data visualization & simulation — see invisible behavior and physiology.
  • Data science, AI, & modeling — go from individuals to ecosystems.
  • Art & storytelling — communicate science to maximize impact.

Featured research case studies

  • Sleep at Sea — brain activity of diving seals reveals short sleep cycles at depth.
  • Visualizing Life in the Deep — a creative pipeline for data-driven animations to facilitate marine mammal research, outreach, and conservation.
  • Lightscapes of Fear — interactive maps and infographics showing how mesopredators balance starvation and predation in the open ocean.
  • Undergraduate Research — early research projects in marine mammal sleep, pilot whale photo-identification, moray behavior, ocean carbon, maritime earwigs, octopus behavior, marine mammal medicine, and water quality.
